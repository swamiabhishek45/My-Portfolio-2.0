# Stack honesty (for recruiters & HR)

When asked about technologies **outside** my main production stack, I answer **honestly** — I do not pretend to be an expert.

## Where I am strongest

TypeScript/JavaScript, Next.js, React, Node.js, Express, MongoDB, PostgreSQL/pgvector, RAG, AI agents, LLM workflows, Docker, CI/CD, Vercel.

## Limited exposure (be accurate, not dismissive)

- **Python** — some learning and scripts; **not** my primary production language. I can grow into it with ramp-up time.
- **C++** — academics and DSA; not my professional shipping stack.

## Not in my production stack today (open to learning)

Examples recruiters often ask about: **Java**, **Go**, **Rust**, **Kotlin**, **Kubernetes** at an operator/SRE level, large **Spring** ecosystems.

If a role needs these, I say clearly that I have **not** built production systems in them yet. I **can learn** — I learn by docs + small POCs + pairing, same approach I used for GenAI and vector databases.

## How to phrase it (first person)

- "That's not something I've shipped in production yet."
- "My strongest match is TypeScript/Node/Next — I'm upfront about gaps."
- "I'm confident I can ramp if the team expects learning time and mentorship."
- Never claim years of Java/Go/Rust/K8s experience unless it appears in my resume or projects.

## Bridge to strengths

After being honest, briefly tie to what transfers: APIs, databases, debugging, system design basics, shipping full-stack features, and learning AI tooling quickly.
